Our purpose

Grey Wave exists to help people over 55 thrive in the fourth quarter of life: with Jesus, with each other, and with something worth getting up for.

Games are won in the fourth quarter. Nobody sits it out. That's how we think about these years, and it's why this church exists.

1

Faith that still grows

Whether you've followed Jesus for sixty years or never opened a Bible, there is more for you here. Plain teaching, honest questions, and a God who is not finished with you.

What that looks like: a message you can follow every Sunday, and a small group on a weekday morning if you want to go deeper.
2

Friends who show up

Loneliness is the quiet epidemic of later life. Here, people know your name, notice when you're missing, and come to the hospital when the news is bad.

What that looks like: coffee before and after, a table you're expected at, and a phone call when you miss a week.
3

Purpose that outlasts you

You carry decades of skill, stories, and faith. Retirement doesn't retire any of it. We'll help you put it to work in the lives of grandchildren, neighbors, and people who need what you have.

What that looks like: real jobs to do, people to mentor, and a church that leaves something behind.

"They will still bear fruit in old age; they will stay fresh and green."

Psalm 92:14
Sundays

Church the way it should feel at this stage of life.

Warm, simple, and unhurried. Here's what an hour with us looks like.

Songs you know by heart

Hymns and the songs of the last fifty years, sung at a pitch and volume you can actually join. Words in large print, every time.

A message you can follow

One passage of the Bible, explained plainly, applied to real life: grief, grandkids, health, money, regret, and what comes next. About 25 minutes.

Communion every week

We gather around the Lord's table every Sunday, the way the first Christians did. Everyone who belongs to Jesus is welcome at it.

Coffee and a real conversation

We start with coffee and we stay after. Nobody sits alone, and nobody gets hurried out the door to make room for the next crowd.

Good to know

  • About one hour, start to finish
  • Daytime. No driving after dark
  • Level entrance, walkers and wheelchairs welcome
  • Large print and clear sound
  • Rides available if you ask
  • Nobody will single you out or ask for money

Questions people ask

The honest answers.

Do I have to dress up?

No. Sunday clothes, gardening clothes, whatever you'd wear to the diner. Lance usually wears a sport shirt. You will not be underdressed.

I don't hear well. Will I be able to follow?

Yes. We keep the music at a reasonable volume, the speaking clear, and the words on screen in large print. Sit anywhere you like; the front rows are not reserved for anyone. If you use hearing aids with a telecoil, tell us and we'll help.

I use a walker or wheelchair.

The entrance is level, the parking is close, the aisles are wide, and there is space at the end of every row. You will not be a bother. You'll be a regular.

I'm not religious. Or I was raised something else.

You're welcome. Catholic, Lutheran, Baptist, Methodist, lapsed, or "never went." Nobody will quiz you, ask you to stand up, or expect you to know when to sit. We are Christians only, but we are not the only Christians.

My spouse passed away. I don't want to walk in alone.

Then don't. Call Lance and someone will meet you in the parking lot and sit with you. A good number of us are walking the same road, and we're honest about it. Grief has a seat here.

Will you ask me for money?

No. There is no offering plate passed and nobody will call about giving. If you decide later that you want to help, we'll gladly show you how. That's your move, not ours.

Is it only for older people? Can I bring my grandkids?

Grey Wave is built for people 55 and better, but the door is open to anyone. Grandkids, adult children, a neighbor who's 40. Bring whoever you want to bring.

Is this a real church or a program at another church?

A real church. We are getting started, so for now we gather in a building that belongs to a sister church. Same Bible, same Jesus, our own people.

Have a different question? Call Lance. He answers his own phone.

Our story

Lance has spent 45 years starting churches. This one is for his own generation.

Lance Hurley planted his first church in Manteno, Illinois, in 1981, fresh out of Lincoln Christian College, with a core group of 20 people. Nineteen years later that church was 250 strong and more than 400 people had been baptized. He went on to pastor in Florida, plant Cornerstone Christian Church in Glen Ellyn in 2005, and lead Ignite Church Planting, the hundred-year-old network behind dozens of new churches across Chicagoland.

Along the way, he and Darla watched something happen to their friends. As people retired, they drifted from church. Not from God, necessarily. From church. Everything new was built for young families, and nobody was building anything for them.

So Lance decided to plant one more. Grey Wave started this fall with twelve people in the room.

"If you ever start a church, I'll come."A longtime friend of Lance's who had said no to church for years. He was there the first Sunday.

Lance and Darla have been married 42 years. They are in the fourth quarter too, and they intend to play it.

What we believe

Simple faith. Nothing hidden.

Grey Wave is an independent Christian church in the same family as Ignite Church Planting and hundreds of Christian churches across Illinois and Indiana. The idea has been the same for two hundred years: get back to the simple church of the New Testament.

  • Jesus alone is Savior and Lord.Not a denomination, not a preacher, not a program.
  • The Bible is our only rulebook.Where it speaks, we speak. Where it's silent, we leave room.
  • No creed but Christ.We ask people to trust and follow Jesus, not to sign a statement.
  • Baptism is for anyone old enough to choose it.By immersion, as the New Testament describes. It is never too late.
  • Communion every Sunday.The Lord's table is the center of our gathering, not an add-on.
  • Led by our own people.Elders from among us, answerable to no headquarters.

"In essentials, unity. In non-essentials, liberty. In all things, love."
